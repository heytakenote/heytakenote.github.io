What is the 19% flat tax?

  • Foreign employees in Korea can choose to pay 19% of their total pay as income tax instead of the regular progressive rates (6% to 45%). With local income tax, that is 20.9%.
  • Under the flat rate you get no deductions or tax credits, and most tax-free allowances are added to your taxable pay.
  • Who can choose it under current law: foreign employees who started working in Korea on or before 31 December 2026, for up to 20 years from the first day of work in Korea.
  • Not available to daily workers, or to people working for a company with a special relationship to them (some exceptions apply, such as certain regional headquarters).
  • You must apply. Ask your employer's payroll team. Your employer can then withhold 19% from your monthly pay.

Proposed change: 19% → 21%

  • The 2026 government tax reform plan proposes raising the flat rate from 19% to 21% and extending the start-date deadline to 31 December 2029.
  • The bill was sent to the National Assembly on 3 September 2026. Tax bills are usually voted on in December, so the final rule and start date may change.
  • We will update this calculator when the law is final.

How the regular tax is estimated here

  • Earned income deduction (근로소득공제) by the legal table, up to ₩20 million.
  • Basic deduction ₩1.5 million per person, and the social insurance you entered.
  • Progressive rates: 6% up to ₩14M, 15% to ₩50M, 24% to ₩88M, 35% to ₩150M, 38% to ₩300M, 40% to ₩500M, 42% to ₩1B, 45% above.
  • Earned income tax credit (근로소득세액공제) with its salary-based limit, and the ₩130,000 standard tax credit.
  • Other deductions (credit cards, medical, education, rent, insurance, children) are not included. If you have them, your regular tax is lower than shown here, so the flat rate becomes less attractive.
