How it is calculated

  • Average daily wage = (pay in the 3 months before you left + bonuses × 3/12 + unused leave pay × 3/12) ÷ number of days in those 3 months.
  • Severance pay = average daily wage × 30 days × (days employed ÷ 365).
  • This tool assumes the same monthly pay in each of the last 3 months. If your pay changed, the official calculator lets you enter each month.
  • If your average wage comes out lower than your ordinary wage (통상임금), the ordinary wage is used instead.
  • Insurance estimate = monthly ordinary wage × 8.3% × months employed. The real amount also includes interest, so ask the insurer for a written statement (they must give one).

Who gets severance pay

  • You worked for the same employer for 1 year or more, and
  • You worked 15 hours or more per week on average (4-week average).
  • Your visa type and nationality do not matter. Foreign workers have the same right as Korean workers.
  • Your employer must pay within 14 days after you leave, unless you both agree on a later date.

E-9 and H-2 workers: departure guarantee insurance

  • Employers of E-9 and H-2 workers must join departure guarantee insurance (출국만기보험) within 15 days of the contract start. It counts as your severance pay.
  • The insurer pays within 14 days after you leave Korea. If you change visa status or apply after leaving, it is 14 days from the application date.
  • You must have worked 1 year or more. If you worked less than 1 year, the money goes back to the employer.
  • If the insurance is less than your legal severance pay, your employer must pay the difference.
  • You must claim within 3 years. After that, unclaimed money is moved to HRD Korea (한국산업인력공단).
